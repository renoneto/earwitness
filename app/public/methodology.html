Methodology

How we transcribe episodes, find ads and mentions, what each label means, and what a result does and doesn’t show.

How we find ads

  1. We turn each episode’s audio into a transcript with timestamps.
  2. Software marks the parts that sound like an ad.
  3. Software names the brand from the words it hears. If it isn’t sure, the ad shows as “Brand not identified”.
  4. Each ad links to its passage in the transcript. Some ads also keep a short audio clip; we don’t keep the full episode audio.

Start and end times are approximate, and a found ad can be wrong. We can also miss ads, so a count is the ads we found, not every ad that ran. Use “Report a mistake” on the ad’s page.

Using EarWitness

  1. Search. Search what was said in the shows we cover.
  2. Open the passage. See the exact words, the episode, the time, the text around it, and where the record came from.
  3. Compare. Put two brands side by side.
  4. Save. Keep passages, searches and bookmarks. A saved passage keeps the transcript version you saved.

Ads and brand names

Ads are found in full-coverage episodes. When we can match an ad to a brand, we show its name. Otherwise the ad shows as “Brand not identified” and doesn’t count toward any brand.

The brand heard and the web address

These are two separate facts. When they disagree we show both and don’t pick one.

Kept separate

  • We keep four things separate: the brand we heard, any web address or code, whether it is an ad, and whether it met your brief.
  • Our own checks on ads are working notes, not accuracy scores.
  • A file fingerprint shows only that two copies match. It is not a legal or authenticity seal.

Uncertainty stays visible

Where audio, timing or requirements are missing, we say so. A match is something heard in the shows we cover, nothing more — see Coverage.

Corrections

Members can report a mistake on any ad. Reports go to the EarWitness team. Only the review team changes what is shown, and a frozen report never changes.
