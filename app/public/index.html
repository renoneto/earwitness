How it works

From a brand name to the moment it was said

Screens from the judges’ pilot, which plays short clips for some ads. Paid plans don’t include clips.

  1. Search any brand or phrase

    Type a brand, a competitor or any phrase. See the ads and mentions we heard, newest first, with the show, the episode and the time.

  2. Open the moment

    See what was said around the ad, and where the brand name was first heard.

  3. Compare and follow

    Put two brands side by side, and get an alert when a word or brand comes up in a new episode.

  4. Check what you bought

    Add the episodes you bought and what the brief asks for. Each episode shows whether the ad was heard and, when the brief names a code, whether it matched. A person confirms each heard ad.

    Example campaign · Ferncastle · brief code FERN15

    • Episode 1Heard, right code
    • Episode 2Heard, wrong code: said FERN40, brief says FERN15
    • Episode 3Not heard in this episode

    Ferncastle is a made-up brand, for illustration.

  5. Ask a question

    The AI part: Ask is off for now. When it is on, Meta’s Muse Spark model does the reading: it answers your questions from the matching passages, and our code checks every quote against its passage and removes the claims that fail those checks.

Method and limits

A match is something heard in the shows we cover, nothing more. Where audio, timing or requirements are missing, we say so.

The words, where they were heard, and when we captured them. A timestamp says where something was heard, not that it was delivered as bought.

Bought a placement? A campaign check compares the ads we found with the brief you supply. It shows what was heard, not proof the ad ran as bought.
